Privacy policy

Fast Lane for Android (com.hrbons.fastlane)

The short version

Fast Lane has no accounts, no ads, no trackers and no analytics. Nothing about you is collected, profiled or sold. The game is played entirely on your own device, and it uses the network for exactly one thing: an online duel against another player, which only happens if you start one. It never asks for your name, and it has nowhere to put one.

What stays on your device

Your two saved games, your statistics, your coin balance and your settings — the sound switch and small interface state, such as whether you collapsed the status bar — live in the app's own preferences files on the device. They never leave it on their own, and uninstalling the app deletes them. The game has no language setting: it simply follows the language your phone is set to, and that choice is never recorded anywhere.

The app allows Android's own backup, so a device backup you have enabled with Google can include your save and your statistics; that is what carries a game in progress to a new phone. That backup is governed by Google's terms, not by this app, and you can switch it off in Android's settings.

One thing is deliberately kept out of that backup: your coin balance. It sits in its own file that both backup routes exclude, so restoring a backup never hands back coins that were already spent.

What leaves your device

Only an online duel, and only after you start or join one. Two phones share a room named by a four-character code that one player reads out to the other. Through that room the app exchanges:

  • the room code itself, the shared random seed, the round number and whether the room is waiting, playing or finished;
  • once a week of game time, each player's position in the game as plain numbers — cash, bank, possessions, education, happiness, career, hunger, the job held, experience, rent tier, study progress, furniture and clothing.

No name, no e-mail address, no device identifier and no identifier this app invented ever goes into a room. There is nothing in a room that says who was playing — only how a game went.

The room lives in a Google Firebase Realtime Database hosted in the EU (europe-west1). Everything goes over HTTPS and the app refuses plain HTTP outright. Like any web service, Firebase sees the IP address the request came from.

A room is protected by its code and nothing else, which is the honest description of a game that has no accounts: someone who knows your code could read or overwrite that room. The database will not hand out a list of rooms, so codes cannot be harvested, and a room holds nothing but the numbers above. The player who opened the room hands it back when they leave the duel. A room whose host never leaves — a force-close, a flat battery — stays behind until it is removed by hand, still holding nothing that names anyone.

When the app hands you to another app

The one link in the game — the voluntary Ko-fi support link — hands you to a browser you pick. From that moment Ko-fi's privacy policy applies, not this one. Fast Lane sends nothing along with it, and it never opens the link without you tapping it.

Children

The game is suitable for all ages, asks for no personal data, and has no chat, no profiles and no public board. Two players can reach each other only by agreeing a room code somewhere else first, and a room carries no text either of them can write.

Deleting your data

Everything the game knows about you is on your phone, so uninstalling it removes all of it. The one thing that ever leaves is a duel room, which the player who opened it hands back on leaving, and which contains no name in any case. If a room was left behind and you want it gone, send its four-character code to the address below and it will be removed by hand.
