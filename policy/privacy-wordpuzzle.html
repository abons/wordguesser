Privacy policy

Word Cross / Woordpuzzel for Android (com.hrbons.wordpuzzle)

The short version

Word Cross has no accounts, no ads, no trackers and no analytics. Nothing about you is profiled or sold. The puzzles are built and played on your own device. The app uses the network to download its word lists, to show the daily leaderboard, and — only when you choose to — to put a name of your own choosing on that leaderboard or to report a name on it.

What stays on your device

Your statistics and day streak, the puzzles you have in progress, your coin balance, the name you last used on the leaderboard and your settings — puzzle language, puzzle size and colour theme — live in the app's own files on the device. So do the downloaded word lists. None of it leaves the device on its own, and uninstalling the app deletes all of it.

The app allows Android's own backup, so a device backup you have enabled with Google can include your statistics, streak and puzzles in progress. That backup is governed by Google's terms, not by this app, and you can switch it off in Android's settings. Your coin balance is deliberately kept out of that backup: it sits in its own file that the cloud backup excludes.

What leaves your device

  • Word lists. On first use, and when you switch the puzzle language, the app downloads its Dutch or English word list and definitions from abons.github.io (GitHub Pages). Nothing about you is sent with that request.
  • Reading the daily leaderboard. When you open it, the app asks for that day's list. Nothing about you is sent with that request either.
  • Joining the daily leaderboard — only if you tap Submit. The app sends the name you type (at most ten letters and digits, reduced to A–Z and 0–9) together with three numbers for that day's puzzle: your time in seconds, your number of mistakes and your number of hints. That entry is public: everyone who opens the leaderboard for that day and language sees the name and the time. Pick a name that does not identify you if you would rather not be recognised.
  • Reporting a name — only if you tap Report. The app sends the reported name, the day and language of the board it is on, and one of three categories. Your own name, your device and any message are not sent; a report carries nothing that says who made it.
  • An update check — in the Google Play version only. At start the app reads a file of a few dozen bytes from abons.github.io that says whether this version may still be played. Nothing about you or your game is sent with it.

The leaderboard and the reports live in a Google Firebase Realtime Database hosted in the EU (europe-west1). Everything goes over HTTPS and the app refuses plain HTTP outright. Like any web service, GitHub and Firebase see the IP address a request comes from; the app itself never stores or sends it, and it sends no device identifier and no identifier of its own.

When the app hands you to another app

Sharing a result opens Android's share sheet with a short text — the puzzle's numbers and the shape of the grid, never its letters — and a link to the web version. Where it goes is up to you and the app you pick. The voluntary Ko-fi support link and the content-policy link open in a browser of your choice, and from that moment that site's privacy policy applies, not this one. The app sends nothing along with either.

Children

The game asks for no personal data. The only thing a player can publish is a leaderboard name, it is never required to play, and names are filtered against a list of offensive words and can be reported from inside the app — see the content policy. There is no chat, no messaging and no profile.

Keeping and deleting your data

Everything on your device goes when you uninstall the app. A leaderboard entry cannot be changed or withdrawn from inside the app — the board is append-only, so that nobody can overwrite someone else's score — and it is kept until it is removed by hand. To have an entry removed, send the name, the day and the language of the board to the address below and it will be deleted; a report you sent is removed the same way once it has been handled.
